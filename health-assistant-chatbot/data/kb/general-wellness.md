# General Wellness FAQs

## How much water should I drink daily?

Most healthy adults do well with roughly 2 to 3 liters (8 to 12 cups) of total fluids per day, including water from food and other beverages. Needs go up with heat, exercise, pregnancy, or illness. A simple check is urine color: pale yellow usually means you're well hydrated, while dark yellow suggests you need more fluids.

## How much sleep do adults need?

Most adults need 7 to 9 hours of sleep per night for healthy cognitive function, mood regulation, and immune health. Consistently sleeping less than 6 hours is linked to higher risk of heart disease, weight gain, and impaired concentration. Keeping a consistent sleep and wake time, even on weekends, is one of the most effective ways to improve sleep quality.

## How much exercise is recommended per week?

General guidelines suggest at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (like brisk walking) or 75 minutes of vigorous activity (like running) per week, plus two sessions of muscle-strengthening activity. Any amount of movement is better than none, and activity can be split into shorter sessions throughout the day.

## What is a balanced diet?

A balanced diet generally includes a variety of vegetables and fruits, whole grains, lean protein sources, and healthy fats, while limiting added sugars, refined carbohydrates, and excessive sodium. Portion control and consistency over time matter more than any single meal.

## How can I manage everyday stress?

Helpful strategies include regular physical activity, adequate sleep, deep-breathing or mindfulness practices, staying connected with friends and family, and limiting caffeine or alcohol when stress is high. If stress feels constant, overwhelming, or is affecting daily functioning, it's worth talking to a healthcare provider or counselor.

## When should I see a doctor for a common cold versus something more serious?

Most colds resolve on their own within 7 to 10 days with rest and fluids. See a doctor if you have a fever above 103°F (39.4°C), symptoms lasting more than 10 days, difficulty breathing, chest pain, persistent vomiting, or symptoms that improve and then suddenly worsen.
