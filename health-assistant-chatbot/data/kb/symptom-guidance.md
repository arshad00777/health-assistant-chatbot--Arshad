# Symptom Guidance (General Information Only)

## Headaches

Most headaches are tension-type or migraine and can often be managed with rest, hydration, over-the-counter pain relief, and reducing screen time or stress. Seek urgent care for a sudden, severe "worst headache of your life," a headache with confusion, stiff neck, vision loss, weakness on one side, or a headache following a head injury.

## Fever in adults

A fever is generally a temperature at or above 100.4°F (38°C). Rest, fluids, and over-the-counter fever reducers such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen (following label dosing) are common first steps. Seek medical care for a fever above 103°F (39.4°C), a fever lasting more than three days, or a fever accompanied by a rash, stiff neck, difficulty breathing, or confusion.

## Sore throat

Most sore throats are caused by viruses and improve within a week with warm fluids, throat lozenges, and rest. See a doctor if the sore throat is severe, lasts longer than a week, comes with a high fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or white patches on the tonsils, as these can indicate a bacterial infection like strep throat that needs treatment.

## Digestive upset (nausea, mild diarrhea)

Mild digestive upset often resolves within 24 to 48 hours with clear fluids, bland foods (like rice, toast, or bananas), and rest. Seek care if you notice blood in stool or vomit, signs of dehydration (dizziness, very dark urine, dry mouth), severe abdominal pain, or symptoms lasting more than a few days.

## Skin rashes

Many rashes are caused by irritants, allergies, or mild infections and improve with gentle skincare and avoiding the trigger. Seek prompt care for a rash that spreads rapidly, is accompanied by fever or difficulty breathing, looks like bruising or bleeding under the skin, or affects a large area of the body.

## When any symptom needs emergency care

Call emergency services or go to an emergency room immediately for: chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or numbness (especially one-sided), sudden confusion or trouble speaking, severe allergic reactions (swelling of the face/throat, difficulty breathing), uncontrolled bleeding, or thoughts of harming yourself or others.
