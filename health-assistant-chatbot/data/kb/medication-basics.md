# Medication Safety Basics (General Information Only)

## Taking over-the-counter pain relievers safely

Acetaminophen and NSAIDs (like ibuprofen or naproxen) are common over-the-counter pain relievers. Always follow label dosing, avoid combining multiple products that contain the same active ingredient, and check with a pharmacist or doctor before combining with other medications, alcohol, or if you have liver, kidney, or stomach conditions.

## Common drug interactions to be aware of

Some well-known interaction categories include: blood thinners with NSAIDs (increased bleeding risk), certain antibiotics with dairy or antacids (reduced absorption), and grapefruit juice with some cholesterol or blood pressure medications (altered drug levels). This is general awareness information, not a substitute for checking your specific medications with a pharmacist.

## Storing medications properly

Most medications should be stored in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, not in a bathroom medicine cabinet where humidity is high. Keep medications in their original, labeled containers and out of reach of children and pets. Check expiration dates periodically and dispose of expired medication through a pharmacy take-back program when possible.

## Missed a dose — what generally helps

If you miss a dose of a regularly scheduled medication, general guidance is to take it as soon as you remember unless it's almost time for the next dose, in which case you typically skip the missed one rather than doubling up. Because this varies by medication, checking the specific instructions for your prescription or asking a pharmacist is the safest approach.

## When to talk to a pharmacist or doctor before starting a new medication

Always mention all current medications, supplements, and allergies before starting something new. This is especially important for anyone who is pregnant or breastfeeding, has chronic kidney or liver disease, or is already taking multiple prescription medications.
